# Codebook for Semi-Structured Interviews

This document presents the codebook used to analyze the semi-structured interviews conducted in our user study. Each entry lists the code, its definition, and example quotes from participants (P01–P30). Quotes are English translations of interviews conducted in Japanese.

## C01 — Sound Source

**Definition:** Statements about the perceived source of an AVMA stimulus (VR vs. the physical lab). Includes which source the participant attributed the sound to and the reasoning behind it. Also includes whether the participant did or did not misattribute the sound to the physical environment.

**Example quotes:**

- "I thought it was coming from the VR headset." (P16)
- "Oh, I thought it was outside, so I didn’t think it was playing inside the VR game." (P30)

## C02 — Sound Realism

**Definition:** Statements about the perceived realism of an AVMA stimulus. Includes evaluations of sound quality, whether it sounded recorded, and how authentic it felt.

**Example quotes:**

- "It sounded pretty real." (P03)
- "Yeah, I kind of felt it might be a synthesized voice." (P26)

## C03 — Attack / Malicious Intent

**Definition:** Statements about whether the participant perceived an AVMA stimulus as an attack or as malicious. Includes evaluations of intent and perceived harmfulness.

**Example quotes:**

- "Rather than malicious, I thought it was a warning sound like the game was in trouble, so I just felt rushed." (P14)
- "I didn’t really feel any malicious intent." (P16)

## C04 — Psychological Impact

**Definition:** Statements about psychological reactions or emotions induced by an AVMA stimulus. Includes surprise, anxiety, discomfort, annoyance, and whether it felt distracting or not.

**Example quotes:**

- "I was startled." (P09)
- "For a moment, I felt this tightness, like my heart clenched a bit." (P22)

## C05 — References to the Physical Environment

**Definition:** Statements grounded in the situation of the lab or the physical environment in relation to an AVMA stimulus. Includes remarks such as "There shouldn’t be a baby in this room" or "I didn’t think that sound would occur here," reflecting incongruity inferred from the environment.

**Example quotes:**

- "I thought someone had just come in from over there." (P03)
- "Well, I figured there probably isn’t a baby around here." (P14)

## C06 — Conditions for Induction While Wearing an HMD

**Definition:** Statements about conditions under which auditory stimuli could induce viewpoint shifts while wearing an HMD. Includes descriptions of when induction would or would not occur, such as "I would turn around for a sound like X."

**Example quotes:**

- "If it didn’t feel like it was coming from inside the game, I think I might turn around." (P04)
- "If it sounded like a voice close to someone I know, I feel like I’d turn around." (P21)

## C07 — Task Impact of Audio

**Definition:** Statements about how an AVMA stimulus affected task performance. Includes references to pausing, reduced concentration, interruptions, and perceived changes in performance.

**Example quotes:**

- "Yeah, like in Tetris, I ended up placing it in the wrong spot." (P09)
- "It felt like my hands stopped for a moment." (P16)

## C08 — Perceived Direction of the Sound

**Definition:** Statements about the perceived direction of an AVMA stimulus, i.e., sound-image localization.

**Example quotes:**

- "The 'Good job' sounded from here on the right, and the baby and dishes were front-left." (P05)
- "'Good job' felt like it came from the left, but I’m not totally sure." (P20)

## C09 — Expected Reaction in Everyday Life

**Definition:** Statements about how the participant would react to a similar sound in everyday life rather than in the experiment or while wearing the HMD. Includes predictions of behavior such as whether they would turn around.

**Example quotes:**

- "For the vibration and the sound when blocks disappear, I don’t think I would turn around." (P07)
- "For 'Good job,' if I heard it at school, I think I’d look." (P15)

## C10 — Fatigue During the Study

**Definition:** Statements about physical or mental fatigue experienced during participation. Includes burden related to task duration or wearing the HMD.

**Example quotes:**

- "My eyes got tired." (P04)
- "Mostly, the headset was heavy and my neck got tired." (P21)

## C11 — Task Engagement

**Definition:** Statements about concentration and immersion in the task. Includes evaluations such as being focused on the task or finding it enjoyable as a game.

**Example quotes:**

- "I was playing Tetris, probably having fun, the whole time." (P08)
- "I was basically focused on the game." (P13)

## C12 — Head Turn / Gaze Shift

**Definition:** Statements about whether the participant actually turned around or shifted their gaze in response to an AVMA stimulus, and explanations for why viewpoint manipulation did or did not occur.

**Example quotes:**

- "I don’t really remember turning around." (P13)
- "It was unconscious. The sound played and I was like 'Huh?' and just looked." (P24)

## C13 — Attributed to Bugs or System Sounds

**Definition:** Statements interpreting an AVMA stimulus as a game bug or as an OS/system notification sound.

**Example quotes:**

- "I thought the cheering was just part of the game’s sound effects, so it didn’t feel unnatural." (P12)
- "For that one, I just thought something glitched or something." (P25)

## C14 — Experiment Bias

**Definition:** Statements indicating that participation in an experiment itself, the experimental design, or system settings (e.g., volume, reverberation, perceived distance) influenced the participant’s reactions. Includes remarks such as "Because it was an experiment, I thought X."

**Example quotes:**

- "Because it sounded kind of distant, like a recording." (P14)
- "I thought it might be a sound set up as part of the experiment." (P22)

## C15 — Sound Misrecognition

**Definition:** Statements indicating that the participant remembered or recognized a different sound than the one presented, or reported hearing a sound that was not presented.

**Example quotes:**

- "There was like a 'meow' sound." (P10)
- "Like a siren, I feel like I heard it, or maybe not. I’m not confident, but I remember picturing a police car or an ambulance." (P24)

## C16 — Most Impactful / Most Memorable Sound

**Definition:** Statements about which AVMA stimulus was most impactful on viewpoint manipulation or the task, or which sound was most memorable, and why. Purely relative comparisons (e.g., "more memorable than X") are excluded.

**Example quotes:**

- "The most memorable were the baby crying and the alarm sound." (P02)
- "It stood out because it felt like it was said to me." (P08)

## C17 — Least Impactful / Not Memorable Sound

**Definition:** Statements about which AVMA stimulus had little impact on viewpoint manipulation or the task, or was hardly memorable, and why. Purely relative comparisons are excluded.

**Example quotes:**

- "The cat sound felt like a cat, and I found it kind of soothing, so it didn’t affect me much." (P01)
- "Among what I heard, the glass sound didn’t leave much of an impression." (P23)

## C18 — Unnoticed Sound

**Definition:** Statements about a sound for which the participant reported having no memory of hearing it or not remembering it played, despite the stimulus being presented.

**Example quotes:**

- "I don’t remember." (P04)
- "If you say so, maybe it played, but I don’t clearly remember. Maybe because I was tired from the game." (P04)

## C19 — Unnoticed Response

**Definition:** Statements made when the participant claimed "I didn’t move" or "I don’t remember," but a response such as viewpoint movement was observed and the participant was given feedback about it. Includes remarks about unrecognized reactions and interpretations.

**Example quotes:**

- "I don’t remember." (P16)
- "Oh, really?" (P19)
